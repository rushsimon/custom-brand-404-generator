# Creative selection and Anti-Template Test

## Derive concepts

Combine brand character + product/category behavior + user scenario + missing-page context. Derive 3–5 materially different candidates, not copy variants of the first idea. Each needs an evidence-backed premise, copy direction, visual expression, recovery path, optional interaction and feasible low-cost implementation. Derive composition and hierarchy from each brand’s evidence rather than reusing one layout across projects; changing only logo, palette or product imagery does not count as a distinct experience. A restrained typographic composition may be the right creative solution; no universal hero layout, floating “404”, mascot or game.

Set intensity from evidence: premium brands may favor precision and subtle motion; technical brands may support meaningful system states; youth brands may welcome play; B2B usually benefits from rapid product/solution recovery. These are hypotheses to verify, not industry prescriptions.

## Evaluate internally

Score each candidate 1–5 on brand fit, product relevance, originality, user understanding, usability, technical feasibility, performance economy (5 = low cost), visual potential and interaction potential. Record brief evidence and tradeoffs. Interaction potential measures whether the chosen level is appropriate; a static design may score highly.

Reject candidates that obscure what happened, lack a real recovery destination, require unsupported claims/assets, fail HTTP requirements or cannot fit the repository without disproportionate changes. For remaining candidates use brand fit, understanding and usability to break close scores; do not let a numeric total reward spectacle over recovery.

## Mandatory Anti-Template Tests

Apply to every shortlisted candidate, then to the final implemented experience:

1. **Identity removal:** remove logo, name and primary color mentally. If only a generic 404 remains, redesign its premise or expression.
2. **Generic copy:** if the idea is just “Oops”, “Lost”, “Broken” or “Page Not Found” with brand styling, redesign. A clear literal error explanation may accompany a distinctive concept.
3. **Grounding:** identify concrete uses of at least two of product behavior, product context, user scenario and brand character. Fewer than two lowers the score and requires improvement before selection.
4. **Competitor substitution:** if a competitor's logo makes the whole experience work unchanged, strengthen it using evidenced brand-specific product details, visual grammar, vocabulary or interactions. Do not claim proprietary uniqueness from a category metaphor alone.

The concept should remain intelligible without a logo and recognizable at least at category level. Reject an experience that could be dropped unchanged onto ten competitors. Brand recognition is an aspiration grounded in evidence, never an excuse to invent product capabilities.

If a test fails, revise or replace the candidate and retest. After two unsuccessful concept rounds, simplify the expression using the strongest verified brand/scenario evidence, explicitly reassess the tests and disclose any remaining specificity limitation. If no candidate passes after that reassessment, report the concept as unresolved and do not implement a failed candidate as the final design. Do not loop forever or silently declare a failed test passed.

## Final choice

Select the strongest viable concept independently. Keep the other candidates internal unless multiple concepts or a detailed report were requested. Explain the chosen premise in 1–3 sentences and tie its fit to observed brand/product details.
