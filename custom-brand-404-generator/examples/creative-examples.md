# Derivation examples, never reusable page templates

Never copy an example concept directly merely because the product category is similar.

These examples teach product behavior → metaphor → 404 scenario → expression/interaction. They are hypothetical, not evidence about any real brand. Re-derive all concepts from the target's actual brand, products and audience. No example dictates a layout, headline, palette or mandatory animation.

## Drone: distinguish category from brand

Behavior: a controller loses connection. Metaphor: a route cannot be reached. A plain “Lost Signal” screen fits many drone brands and fails competitor substitution. For a hypothetical brand with an evidenced mapping-first interface, terrain contour language and return-to-home terminology, a missing waypoint could be expressed through its actual mapping vocabulary and visual grammar. The return control is an ordinary working home/category link; no game or reconnection delay gates recovery. Do not imply the real drone or service has malfunctioned.

## Robot vacuum: change with the audience

Behavior: a device explores a room. Metaphor: a mapped area is unavailable. For an evidenced warm household brand, a small recognizable room-plan detail may support gentle humor. For a commercial fleet brand, the same “under the sofa” joke is inappropriate: an unmapped zone with precise fleet/docs recovery may fit better. Distinct audience and brand character change copy, composition and intensity despite the shared category.

## Tennis training: avoid mandatory gamification

Behavior: balls cross court boundaries. Metaphor: the requested destination is out of play. A youth training brand might use a brief ball movement, while a precision coaching brand might express a missing target through its real calibration markings and terminology. Neither should require the user to hit a ball to navigate. A generic court plus swapped logo is insufficient; use evidenced product behavior and brand details or choose another premise.

## Restrained B2B: creativity can be static

A hypothetical industrial parts catalog emphasizes measured tolerances and annotated drawings. A missing-reference annotation in its existing drawing style can explain a nonexistent part/page and offer real catalog/support routes. No invented part specification, outage claim or fake quote. Brand-specific typography, annotation conventions and terminology carry the idea; “power outage” is not automatically suitable merely because the company sells generators.

## Negative case and correction

Rejected: giant “404”, “Oops!”, random astronaut, brand-colored button. Removing identity leaves a generic template; product/scenario grounding is absent.
Correction process: inspect an actual product interaction and audience task; identify a missing-state analogy; express it using observed visual/copy conventions; retain immediate real recovery links; score alternatives and rerun all tests. The correction is a method, not a preselected illustration.
